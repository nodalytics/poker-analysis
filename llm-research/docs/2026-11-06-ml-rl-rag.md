## Reinforcement learning (`scripts/rl_agent.py`)

Tabular Monte Carlo control (state: street, equity band, facing a bet, price, position, depth; actions: fold,
check/call, half pot, pot, all-in), trained 60 iterations at short stacks against the fast bots. The mean reward
rose from −1.19 to about +0.3 big blinds a decision (`results/rl_short/curve.json`). Then 300 greedy sessions:

| Seat | bb/100 (95% interval) |
|---|---|
| tina | +28.2 (+16.7, +41.8) |
| **rlbot** | **+27.5 (+11.6, +43.4)** |
| monte, tinaplus, bob | +12.6 to +13.4 |
| solverbot | +8.8 (−2.0, +20.2) |

The RL agent beat the solver bot here, but it trained against these exact opponents: it learned to exploit them,
while the solver plays a balanced strategy tuned to no one. The fair test is unseen opponents. AlphaPoker on Leduc
shows the same agent type does not converge in self-play.

## RAG and embeddings (`scripts/rag_eval.py`, `results/rag/report.json`)

Embedding models: nomic-embed-text, mxbai-embed-large, bge-m3 (Ollama on the lab).

**Questions about our own research (12 questions, answers stated in docs/):** both Qwen and Tiger-Gemma 9B answered
0 of 12 alone and 11 of 12 with the 4 retrieved chunks. Retrieval found the answer for 11 of 12 with mxbai and
bge-m3, 10 with nomic.

**Predicting the solver's flop action by retrieving similar solved spots (600 queries on 25 held-out flops):**

| Method | Top action agrees with the solver |
|---|---|
| Trained models (`ml_flop.py`) | 81-83% |
| 5 nearest spots by hand-made features | 74.5% |
| 5 nearest by embeddings (nomic / bge-m3 / mxbai) | 67.7% / 65.3% / 63.5% |
| Decision-point average | 61.5% |

**The LLM on those spots (120 queries):** Qwen 29.2% alone, 27.5-30.8% with retrieved spots; Tiger-Gemma 9B 37.5%
alone, 36.7-43.3% with them. Both are worse alone than the plain average, and with 5 solved spots in the prompt
they still do far worse than averaging those spots (74.5%). RAG works for knowledge; for decisions the retrieval or
the solver should decide.

## Hand histories: all of HandHQ

`scripts/hand_histories.py` over every site and stake: **21,606,087 hands** (`results/handhist_full/`). Players
tighten and get more aggressive as stakes rise: pre-flop raise 10-12% at 25NL to 18-20% at 1000NL, 3-bet about 4%
to 8-9%, while VPIP stays 23-31%.

## Predictive analysis, ESN and PCA (`scripts/predictive.py`, `results/predictive/`)

1.3M HandHQ hands (up to 60,000 a folder, replayed with PokerKit to exact results), split by time: profiles from
the first 40%, training on the next 40%, testing on the last 20%.

**Next action (fold / check-call / bet-raise), 100,000 test decisions:**

| Model | Accuracy | Log loss |
|---|---|---|
| Majority per street | 62.6% | 0.908 |
| Logistic regression | 66.7% | 0.772 |
| ESN, action sequence only | 67.2% | 0.760 |
| ESN + table features | 68.2% | 0.729 |
| MLP | 68.9% | 0.703 |
| Random forest | 69.1% | 0.701 |
| Gradient boosting | **69.2%** | **0.699** |

The echo state network, a fixed random reservoir reading the hand's earlier actions with only a linear read-out
trained, beats logistic regression from the sequence alone; trees and the MLP lead by about one point.

**PCA of 2,624 players' styles (500+ hands each):** PC1 (52% of the variation) is looseness and pre-flop aggression
(VPIP, PFR, 3-bet up, folding to bets down); PC2 (28%) is post-flop aggression. Win rate correlates with every
component at |r| ≤ 0.08: winners and losers overlap everywhere.

**Does style predict profit?** For 1,107 players with 1,000+ hands, style from the first half of their hands against
win rate in the second half: cross-validated R² −0.008 (linear) and −0.31 (gradient boosting). Even a player's own
first-half win rate does not predict the second half (r −0.02). Over about 500-hand halves, results are noise; it
takes far more hands than this dataset has per player to tell a winner by results or by style.

## What it adds up to

1. The solver should decide, at every street; the LLM should read and feed it. Significant at both depths now.
2. Trained models beat retrieval, and both beat the LLM, for poker decisions.
3. Self-play must average (AlphaPoker); greedy RL exploits a known table but does not converge to a balanced one.
4. Opponent modelling from history reaches about 69% on next actions; style alone does not mark winners.
