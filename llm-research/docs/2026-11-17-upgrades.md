# Upgrades beyond more hands (2026-11-17)

About 104 billion training hands across all runs are enough: more of them no longer helps. nm26 is nm25 trained 12
more hours and ties it (+1.03 against +0.96 on the ladder, and more exploitable: +1.1 against +0.2); nm20, nm25, nm26
and nm27 sit within 0.08 bb/100 of each other after very different amounts of training. The gains came from what and
how the networks learn (the ante and short tables, NeuRD's settings) and from search on top (+3.7 to +6.2 bb/100,
the largest). These are the upgrades in that direction, each with how it is judged. Training runs are pre-registered
in `results/prereg/` before they start.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Search distilled into the network (expert iteration) | training: nm36 | data generating, then 6 h; registered |
| 2 | Belief modelling as an auxiliary target | training: nm38 | queued (6 h); registered |

## 1. Search distilled into the network (nm36)

Expert iteration, as AlphaZero and ReBeL train: positions from nm25's own self-play, where Kestrel would search (before
the flop, and up to three players after it), each labelled by search with 2,048 deals: every legal move's value Q and
nm25's policy (`scripts/ninemax_distill_data.py`; 600,000 positions on three GPUs). Training adds to nm25's usual loss
the cross-entropy towards the improved policy

$$\pi^*(a) \propto \pi_{nm25}(a)\, e^{(Q(a) - \max Q)/\tau}, \qquad \tau = 1 \text{ bb}$$

(`ninemax_selfplay.py --distill`). Search disagreed with nm25's top choice in 29% of the smoke test's positions:
those are what is learned. If nm36 wins, the loop repeats (search with nm36, distil into the next).
Judged (registered): a sequential match against nm25 at alpha 0.025 (a family of two with nm38), and the exploiter.

## 2. Belief modelling (nm38)

An extra output, used only in training, predicts each live opponent's hand strength in tenths from the last hidden
layer (`--belief-weight 0.5`). The policy does not read it; the point is that the shared layers must then carry the
opponents' ranges, which the reward alone teaches slowly. DeepStack and ReBeL reason over ranges explicitly; this is
the cheap version. The exporter leaves the head out, so Kestrel loads the model unchanged. Smoke test: the belief
loss starts at 1.59 (guessing would be 2.30: the betting already says a lot) and falls. Judged as nm36.

## 3. Safe exploitation (Kestrel, `search-exploit`)

Kestrel's profiles (types, an empirical-Bayes prior, recent play) now feed play. For each opponent, `opponent_tilt`
turns three tendencies into factors on the network's fold, call and raise probabilities, from the z of the player's
rate against the platform's population (clamped to ±2, scaled by 0.5):

| Factor | From |
|---|---|
| fold | fold to a continuation bet |
| call | went to showdown, less post-flop aggression (a calling station calls down) |
| raise | post-flop aggression and pre-flop raising |

Each factor is exp(w · 0.5 · z) with w = n / (n + 100): a player seen for 10 hands gets factors near 1, which is the
network itself. Search plays every deal a second time with each opponent tilted so, and a move's value becomes

$$Q(a) = 0.5\, Q_{\text{network opponents}}(a) + 0.5\, Q_{\text{profiled opponents}}(a)$$

so with no evidence nothing changes, and with plenty the decision leans half-way towards the profile; it never relies
on the profile alone (data-biased responses, Johanson and Bowling; the "weight by the evidence" principle of
`kestrel/docs/design.md`). Offered as the solver `search-exploit` (web and desktop new-table dialog, `/v1/sessions`,
`kestreld play --solver search-exploit`; `/v1/decide` takes the tilts as `"exploit": {player: {fold, call, raise}}`).
Judged: against `search` on the same test-platform lineups and seeds (bots with known leaks), and it must not lose
against copies of nm25, where every tilt is near 1.

## 4. Real human hands

Every detector and profile here was checked on bots, which are more regular than people. Open Poker (6-max cash, a
WebSocket API) is the first platform with human players; it waits on the sign-in link sent to the account's address.

## 5. Coverage

- **Deeper stacks (nm39):** our tournament models train at 10-25bb; the early stages of a tournament are 25-60bb.
  nm39 is nm25 trained there for 6 hours. Judged in its own game against nm25 (ahead or equivalent), with the copy
  check, the exploiter and the 20bb sense checks; it would ship as the deep-stack option beside nm25.
- **6-max cash (nm37):** nm28's hole (+11.0 to the exploiter) shrank to +7.0 with the league (nm35) and +8.2 with a
  bigger exploiter (nm34), neither closed. nm37 continues nm35's league for 12 hours on the faster GPU.

## 6. Ensemble distillation (nm40)

nm20, nm25, nm26 and nm27 tie on strength but differ in style (the ladder is not transitive, chi²/dof 1.65). Their
average policy, distilled into one network, is usually stronger and harder to exploit than any member. The same
pipeline as 1, without search: 3,012,857 positions from nm25's self-play, every decision labelled with the four
networks' average policy (Q = 0, so the target is the average). nm40 = nm25 trained towards it, 6 hours. Judged as
nm36.

## 7. Cheaper evaluation

Evaluation, not training, is now the bottleneck. Done: group-sequential tests (`seqtest.py`, 20-83% of deals saved
at held error rates). Designed, not built: AIVAT-style action terms. At each opponent decision, add f(s, a) −
Σ_a' π(a'|s) f(s, a') for a value estimate f; it has mean zero exactly (the opponent's policy is known), so it can be
subtracted without bias, and with f the value network's estimate of the hero's result after each move it removes the
luck of the opponents' sampled moves, which card-luck terms could not (×1.00).
